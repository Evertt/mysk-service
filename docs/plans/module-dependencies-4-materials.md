# Module dependencies, part 4: materials as a module — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Status:** implemented on branch `materials-module` (October 2026), for review before merging. Decisions 1–6 change what a tenant sees; the user accepted each recommendation (October 2026).

**Goal:** The core materials domain becomes the optional module `src/modules/materials/`: tenants can switch it off, existing tenants keep it, and core knows nothing about materials. It's the second "core feature becomes a module" pilot, and the first with tables of its own that core used to read and write: order lines, the stock ledger, and the pricing, forms and pages built on them.

**Architecture:** The three tables (`material`, `order_line`, `stock_movement`) move, unchanged, to the module's schema; core keeps `order.laborHours` and the order's status, and stops joining order lines anywhere. Everything core did with materials becomes a contribution to an extension point core declares:

- **Prices:** `priceOrders()` asks a new collector, `orders.costs`, what an order costs besides labour (the materials module answers with the order lines' total, and, when asked for them, the lines themselves for display), before it asks `orders.adjustPrices`. `materialsPerOrder()`, `lineTotalSql()` and every caller's own materials subquery go.
- **Saving:** a new kind of hook, the _save hook_, runs inside the owner's transaction. Core calls `orders.saving` wherever an order is created, updated, deleted or changes status. The module saves the order form's material lines there, and takes or returns stock as the status says.
- **Pages:** the order form gets a form slot (`orders.form`) where the module renders its lines card, submitted with the order; the dashboard's headline row gets a slot (`dashboard.stats`) for the low-stock card; the engineer portal's order page gets a slot (`portal.order`) for the materials card. The order page and a unit's service history render the costs' items generically.
- The materials pages (`/materials`, `/materials/new`, `/materials/[sku]`, `/materials/[sku]/edit`), its components, remote functions, messages, activity subjects and verbs, enum labels, number format and seed move into the module. The sidebar entry comes from the manifest (`nav: { after: "engineers" }`, the same place as today).

**Tech Stack:** SvelteKit 3 (remote functions), Svelte 5, TypeScript, drizzle (SQLite), valibot, Vitest (+ vitest-browser-svelte, Playwright e2e), pnpm, Vite+ (`vp`).

**Spec:** [`docs/plans/module-dependencies.md`](module-dependencies.md): section 2 (read tables, write through services; a module's tables reference core and what it requires), section 3 (extension points their owners declare; new points when a module needs them) and section 5 (a migration keeps the feature for existing tenants). Template: [step 3, the schedule](module-dependencies-3-schedule.md).

## Decisions

### Decided by the user

1. **The order page with materials on.** Decided (the recommendation): **unchanged.** The price card lists the order's material lines as today, then labour, the adjustments and the total; it renders whatever `orders.costs` reports with its items, so core doesn't know they're materials. Visible differences: the first column's heading becomes "Item" (it can no longer say "Material"), and the card's title is made from the costs' names ("Materials & labour"; "Labour" without materials). Alternative: the lines move to a card of their own that the module adds (a new `orders.detail.main` slot), and the price card shows one "Materials" row.
   **With materials off:** the card shows labour, the adjustments and the total, titled "Labour".
2. **The order form with materials on.** Decided (the recommendation): **unchanged.** The module's materials card sits in the order form (the `orders.form` slot), is submitted with it, and is saved with the order in one transaction, refusing a shortage on its line as today. Alternative: materials leave the order form, and are added on the order page in the module's card, saved on their own (like the portal's); a new order then gets its materials after it's created.
   **With materials off:** the form has no materials card.
3. **Existing organizations keep materials.** Decided (the recommendation): **yes**, by migration (`drizzle/0005_enable-materials.sql`, after main's `0004_add-maintenance`), like the schedule.
4. **New organizations.** Decided (the recommendation): **only when asked**, `org:create --modules materials,…`, like the schedule (the user decided "nothing is on by default for a new organization" then).
5. **The demo tenants.** Decided (the recommendation): **both keep materials**, so the demo data doesn't change at all, and the module's e2e makes "a tenant without materials" by switching it off for South on the settings page and on again at the end (as the schedule's tenancy test does the other way round). Alternative: South loses materials. South's orders stay the same (the seed still draws the materials' randomness for it, see Decision 14), but they have no lines, nothing is in stock, and its prices are labour only; the core e2e that use South's materials (`orders.tenancy.test.ts` and others) change with it.
6. **Switching materials off while orders have lines.** Decided (the recommendation): **allowed, and the data is kept.** While it's off, prices leave the materials out (core asks only the modules that are on), the lines and the stock stay stored, and a status change moves no stock. When it's switched on again, prices include them again, and an order's next status change or save puts its stock right (what an order holds is read from the ledger, Decision 11). Alternative: refuse switching it off while any order has lines.

### Technical: decided here

7. **No real `requires`.** Nothing needs materials to work: contracts covers labour and costs, invoicing bills prices, the schedule plans orders. So the e2e of the settings page's dependency prompts stays carried to the first module with a real `requires` (README "Carried from step 1"). Contracts gets `enhances: ["materials"]` for its seed only (Decision 14).
8. **The tables move unchanged.** `material`, `order_line` and `stock_movement`, with their columns, indexes and foreign keys, move from `src/lib/server/db/schema.ts` to `src/modules/materials/server/schema.ts`, with their relations. `orderRelations` loses `lines`; the module's `orderLineRelations` and `stockMovementRelations` keep their `order` side. The same table definitions make no DDL, which `pnpm db:generate` confirms (no migration besides Decision 3's data migration); `modules.spec.ts` accepts them (they reference `order` and `user`, both core, and their own). `moveStock()` stays the only writer of `material.stock`.
9. **Prices: the collector `orders.costs`** (`onError: "fail"`), in `#lib/orders/extensions.ts`:

   ```ts
   /** What an order costs besides labour, as one of the tenant's modules adds it, e.g. its materials. */
   export interface OrderCost {
   	label: string; // "Materials"
   	amount: number; // cents
   	/** What it's made of, when the caller asks for items: e.g. one per material line. */
   	items?: OrderCostItem[];
   	/** Shown when it has no items, e.g. "No materials". */
   	empty?: string;
   }
   export interface OrderCostItem {
   	key: string;
   	label: string;
   	href?: string;
   	quantity: string; // formatted, e.g. "2.5 m"
   	unitPrice: number;
   	amount: number;
   }
   export const costs = defineCollector<
   	{ orders: OrderFacts[]; items: boolean },
   	Map<number, OrderCost>
   >("orders.costs", { onError: "fail" });
   ```

   As built, an item links to its record through `subject?: { type, id, label }` (resolved by the activity log's `subjectHref()` on the page), not an `href`: `resolve()` can't run in a remote query (see Wrinkles). `priceOrders(orders, { items?, enabled? })` asks `costs` first, then `adjustPrices` with `PricedOrderFacts = OrderFacts & { labor: number; costs: OrderCost[] }`, and returns `OrderPrice = { labor; costs: OrderCost[]; adjustments; total }`. `OrderToPrice` loses `materials`, so every caller drops its subquery. The materials module answers every order it's asked about (an order without lines costs 0 materials), so a tenant with materials sees "No materials" and "Materials and labour" where it does today; it asks the lines' totals in one grouped query filtered to the batch (in chunks under SQLite's variable limit), and the lines themselves only when `items` is asked (the order page and a unit's service history).

10. **Contracts' premium level includes labour and every cost** (today the only cost is materials, so nothing changes; its text stays "Labour and materials"). **Invoicing**'s order line is the order at labour plus costs; its detail is made from the costs' names, "{costs} and labour" ("Materials and labour"), or "Labour" when there are none. Stored invoice texts don't change.
11. **Saving: a new kind of hook, the save hook** (`defineSaveHook<In, Problem>(id)` in `#lib/extensions/define.ts`, kind `"save"`, dispatched by `save(point, input)` in `#lib/server/extensions.ts`, read by the generator like the others). Its contributors run one after another in registry order inside the owner's transaction (the input carries `tx`), and each answers problems or nothing; dispatch stops at the first that answers problems, and the owner refuses the change, which rolls the transaction back. A contributor that throws fails the request, also rolling it back. Not chosen: a vote (votes run in parallel and don't write) or an event (after commit, too late to refuse).
    - **`orders.saving`**, called by one function, `saveOrderParts(change)` in `#lib/orders/server/hooks.ts`, which throws a core `OrderRefused` (with the problems) when a module refuses. As built, its input is smaller than first planned: `{ tx, order: { id, number }, status: OrderStatus | null (null while it's being deleted), form?: Record<string, unknown> (the modules' parts of the order form's submission) }`, since with the ledger (below) a module needs neither the old status nor a note; a problem is `{ path?: (string | number)[]; message }`, a path into the form's fields (`["modules", "materials", "lines", 2, "quantity"]`).
    - Called from `saveOrder()` (with the form's parts, after `createOrders()` or the update), `changeOrderStatus()`, `removeOrder()` (before the order's row goes), `scheduleOrder()` and the portal's status changes. Not from `setOrdersStatus()` (invoicing: completed ↔ invoiced hold the same stock, and it "makes no checks", so a refusal there would have nowhere to go), nor from `createOrders()` (a draft has no parts; the form saves them right after). Their callers catch `OrderRefused` where they caught `OrderStockShortage`: the form puts each problem on its field, the others refuse with its message, the schedule's `plan()` returns it.
    - **What an order holds is read from the stock ledger**, not inferred from its old status: the module sums the order's movements per material and moves the difference to what `heldLines(after)` says. So an order whose status changed while materials was off (Decision 6) is put right at its next change, and deleting an order returns exactly what it took. For an order saved with materials on all along this is the same as today (the seed's ledger matches the lines). The shortage message's "available" is the stock plus what the order holds of it, as today.
12. **The form slot `orders.form`** (`defineSlot<{ fields: ModuleFormFields; order: { id: number } | undefined }>`), rendered in the order form after the order's own card, inside the `<form>`: the form takes a `parts` snippet, which the new and edit pages fill with `<ExtensionSlot>` (a `$lib` component can't import a route's). `orderFields` gains `modules: v.optional(v.record(v.string(), modulePart), {})`, where a part is typed `ModuleFormPart` (strings and numbers, two levels deep, no booleans: SvelteKit's form types reject `unknown` and anything recursive); a module's part's inputs are named under `modules.<id>` through `fields.modules.<id>` (SvelteKit's field proxy takes any path; the module casts it to its own shape once), and the module validates its part with its own valibot schema in its save hook. The materials card loads what it offers and the order's lines (with their quoted prices) with its own remote queries.
13. **The other slots**: `dashboard.stats` (stat cards in the headline row; the module's low-stock card and its `getStockLevels` query), and `portal.order` (cards on an engineer's page of one of their orders, in a new `src/lib/portal/extensions.ts` like the dashboard's; it moves to the portal module's `api/` when the portal becomes one). For the portal card, `moduleRemote()` gains the role `"engineer"` (`requireEngineer()`), and the portal's `fieldActions()` and `findMyOrder()` move from `src/routes/(field)/my/` to `#lib/orders/field.ts` and `#lib/orders/server/field.ts`, so the module's `saveFieldMaterials` keeps the portal's rules (the engineer's own order, while it's in progress).
14. **Seed: the faker sequence stays** (verified: the seeded tables, dumped with a fixed clock, are identical before and after, demo users' random ids aside). Materials are drawn between the equipment and the orders, and the lines right after the orders; moving them to after the core's generation would change every tenant's data. So `ModuleSeed` gains `during: { afterEquipment?(draft), afterOrders?(draft) }`, steps `generateTenant()` runs at those two points, for the tenants that have the module. Module data in the draft lives under `draft.modules[<id>]` (core doesn't know its shape). `generateStockMovements()` keeps its own faker and moves with `materials.ts` and the lines' ranges into `src/modules/materials/server/seed.ts`; the module's `run()` inserts materials, lines and movements with its own id offset (from `previous`, like contracts'). `SeedCollectors` gains `costs(order)`, which invoicing's and contracts' seeds use in place of `price.materials`. Contracts' call-outs keep their line through `draft.modules.materials` (a type import from `#modules/materials/api/seed.ts`), hence `enhances: ["materials"]`. If the user picks "South loses materials" (Decision 5), the `during` steps run for every tenant and only the tenants with the module get rows.
15. **Activity:** `recordOrderLines()` moves to the module; the `material` and `order_line` subjects and the `stock_received`/`stock_adjusted` verbs move to the manifest's `subjects` and `actions`, so old entries read the same with materials off (as contracts' do). The unused `material` reference type goes.
16. **Domain rules and labels** move to the module's root: `model.ts` (units, `fitsUnit`, `quantityStep`, stock levels and thresholds, `lineTotal`, `roundQuantity`, `holdsStock`, `stockMovementKinds`, `materialNumbers` = `MAT-`, 4 digits, used with `nextNumber(tx, { format, table, column })`), `labels.ts` (`materialLabels`, the four enum kinds), `display.ts`, `activity.ts`. Their messages move with them: `messages/<locale>/materials.json` to the module, and the `orders_*`, `portal_*`, `dashboard_*`, `enum_*` and `activity_*` keys about materials into it as `materials_*`, texts unchanged.
17. **Moves, by the module's folders:** `ui/` (`material-link`, `stock-badge`), `list/`, `form/`, `detail/` (stock history and dialogs), `orders/` (the order form's lines card, the order's `costs` answer), `portal/` (the portal card), `dashboard/` (the stats card) and `server/` (`stock.ts`, `materials.ts`, `list-sql.ts`, `lines.ts`, `costs.ts`, `index.ts`, `schema.ts`, `seed.ts`); its contributions in `slots/orders.form/`, `slots/dashboard.stats/` and `slots/portal.order/`; `api/seed.ts` (types for contracts' seed); routes `routes/(app)/materials/…` (route ids `/(app)/(modules)/materials/…`). `src/lib/materials/` goes; `materials` leaves the domains of `domain-boundaries.spec.ts` and `coreNavIds`.

## Was it worth it? An honest assessment

**What the module system gained.**

- Four extension points that are general, not materials-shaped: `orders.costs` (anything an order costs besides labour, with optional line items for display: travel, rentals, subcontractors could use it as is), the **save hook** kind with `orders.saving` (a module saving its part of a core record in core's transaction, and refusing it; the "server `saved`/`refuseChange` hooks" the docs listed as missing), the **form slot** `orders.form` (a module's fields inside a core form, submitted and validated with it), and the slots `dashboard.stats` and `portal.order`. `moduleRemote(id, "engineer")` lets a module serve the engineer portal.
- A tenant can now run without inventory: no materials pages, no lines in the form, labour-only prices and invoices that say "Labour".
- Core is smaller and blind to materials: `models.ts`, the schema, labels, numbering, the activity log, the order form, pricing, the dashboard, the portal and the seed lost every materials-specific line (only comments mention them as an example).
- Stock is more robust: what an order holds is read from the ledger, so a status change made while materials was off (or any drift) is put right at the order's next change.
- The seed can now host a module whose data sits in the middle of core's generation, without changing the demo data.

**What got more complex or indirect for someone reading the order code.**

- Saving an order used to be readable top to bottom: `saveOrder()` called `saveOrderLines()`, which called `moveOrderStock()`. Now `saveOrder()` calls `saveOrderParts()`, which dispatches to whichever modules are on, and the stock logic is found by knowing that the materials module contributes to `orders.saving` (`src/modules/materials/server/index.ts` → `saveOrderMaterials()`). Same for prices: `priceOrders()` no longer shows where the materials amount comes from. Go-to-definition stops at the kernel.
- Errors travel further: a shortage becomes an `OrderStockShortage` in the module, an `OrderProblem` with a path into the form's fields, an `OrderRefused` in core, and an `invalid()` issue in the form; four shapes for what used to be one catch.
- The form's typing has an escape hatch: the module's part arrives as `ModuleFormPart` (loosely typed, validated by the module at save time) and the module casts the field proxy to its own shape. The client no longer knows the lines' schema, so field errors only come back from the server.
- Behaviour that core used to guarantee now depends on registration: if the materials module weren't contributing to `orders.saving`, nothing would fail; stock just wouldn't move. The e2e are what pins it.
- More files and hops for one feature: an order page's lines come from a collector with `items`, linked through the activity log's subject registry; the portal card refreshes through `orders.committed`, gated on the user being an engineer.

**Wrinkles hit on the way.**

- `resolve()` can't run in a remote query (SvelteKit's `remote_request_property`), so cost items carry a `subject` and the page resolves it with `subjectHref()`.
- `getCurrentTenant` sends field engineers to their portal, so `<ExtensionSlot>` (now also on the portal) needed its own query, `getTenantModules`; settings refreshes both.
- SvelteKit's form types reject `unknown` and recursive value types (its "all booleans must be optional" check), hence the explicit two-level `ModuleFormPart`.
- SvelteKit refuses a submission with fields its form didn't create (`form_field_unbound`): good for tampering (a materials part posted to a tenant without the module is refused, a 500 rather than a 400), but it means the "ignored part" path is unreachable in practice.
- With order lines leaving the up-front checks, the order form now reports a wrong customer first and a wrong material only once the order's own fields are right (saving the lines needs the order). One core tenancy e2e changed for it.
- Movements are ordered by the order's lines; reading what an order holds from the ledger changed their order until the lines' order was passed along.
- The seed's materials steps are the first module code that runs inside core's generation; contracts needed `enhances: ["materials"]` only to give its seeded call-outs a material line.
- No real `requires` came out of it: nothing needs materials to work. The settings page's dependency-prompt e2e stays carried.
- The dashboard's headline row is a five-column grid; with materials off it has four tiles and a gap.
- The `orders.committed` handlers of contracts and invoicing now also run for the portal's changes (whose user can't call their office queries); nothing was logged in the e2e, but a guard like materials' would be tidier.
